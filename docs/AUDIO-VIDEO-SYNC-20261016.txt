BLACKBOX AUDIO/VIDEO SYNC FIX
October 16, 2026 - 2.0.0-preview.6 - local Windows 10 testing

FINDING
The user's roughly four-hour recording ended with audio 8.981 seconds longer
than video. A flash/tone fixture also showed increasing offset in the old
forced-CFR mode. This was present in media timing, not just the review slider.

CHANGE
Native video now preserves capture-clock timestamps (IsFixedFramerate=false).
Late frames are not packed into an independent fixed-frame-count timeline.
Hardware H.264 remains requested; FFmpeg still copies video, without a second
video encode. BLACKBOX_NOMINAL_FPS metadata preserves the requested frame-rate
setting for frame controls and export compatibility, without retiming packets.

VERIFICATION
- 312 application/export/storage tests and 41 native/protocol/DSP tests passed.
- Five-minute timed run with system audio, microphone DSP, and pause/resume:
  11 MKVs, 154 matched flashes/1 kHz pulses, 3.5 ms start-to-end drift.
- Real SessionExportService joined the timestamped segments to MKV and MP4
  using video stream-copy. The joined MKV retained all 154 pulse pairs and
  measured -22.5 ms start-to-end drift (below one 30 FPS frame).
- In-app exported-video playback: 299 displayed frames over 5.013 seconds,
  zero lost pictures; 22 frame steps, no decoder reopens, changing pixels,
  and both closed review pages collected.
- Extended live test passed resize, minimize/restore, live preset changes,
  target switch while paused, target closure, restart, and parent disconnect.
  No microphone frames were dropped at the checked points.

LIMITS
This is not another four-hour gameplay soak. Pulse timing measures the system
audio path with microphone mixing enabled, not separate physical-mic speech
alignment. A small final microphone/AAC drain can outlast the last video frame;
it is distinct from cumulative drift. Old recordings are unchanged and cannot
recover missing original capture timestamps automatically.
